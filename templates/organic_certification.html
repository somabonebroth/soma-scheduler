Before an audit — work through in order

Each step builds on the one before it. Steps 1 and 3 have a raw-material side and a finished-goods side — do both. Step 1 is not symmetric: the raw side rewrites the books, the finished-goods side only reports. Finished goods are the certified product that actually leaves the building, so an audit that only proves the raw side proves half the chain.

1♻Rebuild Raw BalancesRewrites the books: resets every lot to its received quantity and replays every completed run, so raw-material balances match what was actually used. 1♻Check Finished Goods for DriftRead-only: shows where recorded stock has drifted from produced minus sold, lot by lot, and what explains each gap. Fix drift with a count (step 3).
2📊Mass BalanceCheck the numbers add up, both sides at once — Opening + Received − Consumed should equal what's on the shelf. Investigate any gap.
3🔍Count Raw MaterialsConfirm it for real — physically count raw materials and compare them to the books. 3🔍Count Finished GoodsPhysically count finished goods and compare them to the books. This is the count that covers your certified product.

Organic Recipe Check —

Every active recipe certified Organic should list only organic-named ingredients (salt and water excepted). Production deducts a raw lot only when its name matches the recipe line exactly, so a line named without "organic" is one that would consume a non-organic lot. Fix it on the recipe card.

Stock Exceptions —

Completed batches recorded as produced with less raw material on file than the recipe required. These are historical markers, not open problems — they do not clear, and a non-zero count is not itself a fault. The day each one happened also raises it on that day's Daily Summary.
